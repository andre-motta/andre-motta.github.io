# Editorial choices and evidence

## Angles that fit this site

Choose the structure that serves the source instead of filling a template:

- A boundary that changes the answer: show what works in one representation and
  fails in another, then explain the test that exposes the difference.
- A design tradeoff: explain the useful simplification, the behavior it must
  preserve, and the cost of respecting that constraint.
- A decision a reader can reuse: develop one actionable rule with an example
  and the condition under which it is insufficient.

For `source-trees-are-not-packages.md`, an angle is the difference between testing
a checkout and testing the artifact users install. Keep individual upstream
proposal statuses dated when mentioned. For `tongs-one-review-inbox.md`, an
angle is a common review inbox whose actions still respect each forge's semantics.
Use these as public calibration sources, not recycled copy for every post.

Read enough of the article to avoid promoting an illustrative recommendation as
an implemented feature. A summary should preserve the distinction even if the
long article has room for more detail.

## Engagement evidence

Research checked September 6, 2026:

- [LinkedIn's March 2026 feed update](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/ImprovingTheFeed)
  describes reducing generic content and engagement bait, and favoring useful
  professional perspectives. It does not establish a guaranteed post formula.
- [LinkedIn on authentic conversations](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/authentic-content-and-conversations)
  describes action against engagement pods and automated comments.
- [LinkedIn's feed measurement explanation](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/feed/putting-members-first-testing-and-measuring-how-content-appears-in-your-feed)
  describes many contextual ranking signals. Do not infer a universal posting
  schedule from unrelated creators' results.

These sources support prioritizing useful, authentic writing. The proposed length,
paragraph style, and absence of default hashtags are editorial choices. Refresh
primary sources when making claims about current platform behavior; routine
drafting from an article does not require new algorithm research.

## Learning from Andre's posts

If requested, keep a private record of article, angle, exact post text, publication
time and timezone, canonical link or requested campaign URL, observation window,
impressions, reactions, comments, saves if available, and measured article visits.
Record unavailable values as missing. Note which comments show substantive
technical discussion without collecting commenter profiles or copying identities.

Compare results after the same elapsed time, such as seven days, as an experiment
choice rather than an asserted optimum. Reactions divided by impressions can be
a useful explicitly defined rate, but not all tools define engagement identically.
Campaign-tagged URLs are optional and require confirmed analytics support. Do
not promise that a tracker preserves each UTM dimension. Analytics attribution is
approximate, and site visits are not LinkedIn impressions or unique people.

Avoid winner claims from tiny samples. Vary one factor where practical, such as
the opening or the article angle, and preserve the substantive argument. A post
that attracts a few useful engineering conversations can be preferable to one
that produces many superficial reactions.
